- Greedy
    Is an algorithmic strategy that takes the option that looks best at that moment
    The objective is to arrive at the best overall solution

    Example:
        subject: need to arrive at 18
        vector<int> coins = {10, 5, 2, 1, 50, 20};
        takes 10 (18 - 10 = 8)
        takes 5 (8 - 5 = 3)
        takes 2 (3 - 2 = 1)
        takes 1 (1 - 1 = 0)
        the other coins are discarded
        
        10 + 5 + 2 + 1 = 18

- Dynamic Programming
    Solving a problem by dividing it into smaller subproblems and saving the calculated results so they can be reused
    avoiding unnecessary recalculations.
    It can use a memo (e.g. a vector) to save data.

- 2D Dynamic Programming
    The same as Dynamic Programming,
    but the memo has two dimensions,
    usually represented as dp[i][j],
    where the state depends on two variables

- Retrograde Analysis
    Instead of simulating the game from the initial position,
    we consider all possible game states and solve them backwards,
    starting from the states whose outcome is already known

    Example:
    
    Known terminal states
        ↓
    Their predecessors
        ↓
    More solved states
        ↓
    Their predecessors
        ↓
    ...
        ↓
    Initial state
